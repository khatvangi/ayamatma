The last essay left a debt and I am here to pay it.

It argued that when a man drops what was allocated to him, the harm that follows is not manufactured out of his absence. It is manufactured by a system already in motion, which does not stop, and which must absorb what he let fall. And it said, in passing and without argument, that what he was carrying — his *svadharma* — is **a load, and not a rank**.

That was asserted. It was not established. And it cannot be left standing on assertion, because the entire account depends on it. If the load is wrongly allocated, then the demerit is wrongly attributed, and the machine grinds a man who was never holding anything. A theory of omission that cannot say **what was yours** is not a theory. It is an accusation with a diagram attached.

So: **what fixes *svadharma*?**

There are two candidate answers, and the tradition has given both, and they are not compatible.

**One:** it is something you **are**. A nature, delivered with you, fixed before you drew breath — and therefore knowable from the fact of your birth.

**Two:** it is something you are **carrying**. A load, allocated to a locus by a system already turning — and therefore knowable only by finding out what is actually resting on you.

These give different answers about every man alive. Only one of them is right. And this is a question in metaphysics, and it is decidable, and I intend to decide it.

---

## A declaration, before I begin, about how this will be settled

This claim has enormous social consequences. I know exactly what they are. Everyone reading this knows what they are.

**They will play no part in the argument, and I want that on the record before the first step is taken.**

If a doctrine is false, it is not false **because** its consequences are ugly. And if it is true, it is not made false by them. An argument that arrives at the answer you wanted, along the road your feelings had already built, is not an argument — it is a feeling wearing a syllogism, and everyone can see through it including you.

The contamination runs in both directions and it is the same contamination. Let social outrage drive the metaphysics and you get apologetics: a tradition scrubbed clean to be presentable. Let social convenience drive it and you get the *dharmaśāstra*: a metaphysics bent to hold a settlement in place. **Both are the same crime**, and they are committed by people who would never sit in the same room.

So I am going to do the thing this civilisation actually trained its people to do, and I am going to do it to a claim it never had the stomach to do it to.

**I am going to ask what it is known by.**

And I will report what the audit says. If it says the nature-answer is sound, I will say so, and the consequences will be what they are, and they will not be my business.

---

## What a *pramāṇa* is, and why this is the sharpest question available

If you have this, skip ahead. I am assuming nothing.

Indian philosophy is built around a single obsession: **how do you know?** Not what do you believe. Not what does the tradition hold. By what **instrument** — applied how, delivering what, and answerable to what.

The instruments are ***pramāṇas***. The Advaitins, taking over the Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā list, accept six.

**Pratyakṣa** — perception. You saw it.

**Anumāna** — inference. You did not see it, but you saw something invariably tied to it. Smoke on the hill; therefore fire.

**Upamāna** — comparison. You are told a *gavaya* resembles a cow; you meet one and recognise it.

**Śabda** — testimony, and pre-eminently the Veda. What no other instrument can reach.

**Arthāpatti** — postulation. Something you already hold will not stand with something else you hold, and the not-standing forces a posit. Devadatta is fat. Devadatta does not eat by day. The two will not go together. Therefore: he eats at night.

**Anupalabdhi** — non-apprehension. How an **absence** is known. The pot is not in the room — and you know it because the pot is the sort of thing that **would have been seen** had it been there.

Now here is the discipline, and it is what makes this a serious civilisation rather than a devout one. **Nothing may be asserted without one.** A Naiyāyika will not let you claim there is a pot on the floor until you name the instrument that delivered it. That is not pedantry. On this account, a man who asserts without a *pramāṇa* has not made a **weak** claim. He has not made a claim. He has made a noise.

So take the nature-answer, and state it in its most exact form, and put the question to it.

**By what *pramāṇa* do you know that *this* man's station is fixed by what he *is*, and that what he *is* was fixed by his own prior conduct?**

Not: is there karma. Not: is there rebirth. Not: is the world ordered.

**This man. This nature. His verdict.** Which of the six delivered it?

## The nature-answer at full strength

I will not knock down a weak version. Here is the strong one, and it has two legs, and both are formidable.

**The first leg is śruti**, and it is not obscure.

> तद्य इह रमणीयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते रमणीयां योनिमापद्येरन् — ब्राह्मणयोनिं वा क्षत्रिययोनिं वा वैश्ययोनिं वा । अथ य इह कपूयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते कपूयां योनिमापद्येरन् — श्वयोनिं वा सूकरयोनिं वा चण्डालयोनिं वा ॥
>
> *tad ya iha ramaṇīyacaraṇā abhyāśo ha yat te ramaṇīyāṃ yonim āpadyeran — brāhmaṇayoniṃ vā kṣatriyayoniṃ vā vaiśyayoniṃ vā | atha ya iha kapūyacaraṇā abhyāśo ha yat te kapūyāṃ yonim āpadyeran — śvayoniṃ vā sūkarayoniṃ vā caṇḍālayoniṃ vā ||*
>
> "Those whose conduct here has been pleasant will quickly reach a pleasant womb — the womb of a brāhmaṇa, a kṣatriya, or a vaiśya. Those whose conduct here has been foul will quickly reach a foul womb — the womb of a dog, a pig, or a *caṇḍāla*."
>
> — Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10.7

That is the text and I am not going to soften a syllable of it.

**The second leg is a philosophical argument**, and it is much better than the people who cite it seem to realise.

Look at the world. Two children are born on the same morning. One into plenty, health, a father who reads to him. The other into a hut, hungry, dead at four. **Neither has done anything.** If there is an ordering intelligence at all, it has just done something monstrous. And the only alternative is that there is no ordering intelligence, and the whole thing is a lottery run by nobody.

The tradition refuses both horns in a single line:

> वैषम्यनैर्घृण्ये न सापेक्षत्वात्तथा हि दर्शयति ॥
>
> *vaiṣamyanairghṛṇye na sāpekṣatvāt tathā hi darśayati ||*
>
> "**Partiality and cruelty — no.** Because [Īśvara acts] **in dependence** [on karma]. For so [scripture] shows."
>
> — Brahmasūtra 2.1.34

And the obvious objection, met in the very next sūtra:

> न कर्माविभागादिति चेन्नानादित्वादुपपद्यते चाप्युपलभ्यते च ॥
>
> *na karmāvibhāgād iti cen nānāditvād upapadyate cāpy upalabhyate ca ||*
>
> "If it be said [that this fails] because there was no distinction of karma [before the world began] — no: **because it is beginningless**. It is possible, and it is also observed."
>
> — Brahmasūtra 2.1.35

That is *arthāpatti*, and it is *arthāpatti* doing exactly what *arthāpatti* is for. The observed unevenness will not stand with a just Īśvara. The not-standing forces a posit. **Therefore: prior karma.**

It is a good argument. I will not pretend otherwise. And everything now depends on being exact about **what it licenses** — because I think it licenses something, and I think it is not what has been taken from it.

---

## What the argument delivers — and the exact place it stops

Read the sūtra again and ask a simple question about it.

***Vaiṣamya.*** Partiality. ***Nairghṛṇya.*** Cruelty.

**Whose?**

Not the man's. **Īśvara's.**

The sūtra is not an argument about any individual. It is an **acquittal of God**. Its *explanandum* is **the distribution** — the whole unevenness, taken together — and its conclusion is precisely this and nothing beyond it: **the distribution is not arbitrary.** There is an order in it. Īśvara is not partial and not cruel, because He is not its sole author; He acts *sāpekṣa*, in dependence on something already there.

That is what the *arthāpatti* delivers. **It delivers an order.**

**It does not deliver a verdict on a single soul standing inside it.**

And I must be exact about **what kind of failure** that is, because if I am loose here the whole essay is worthless.

It is **not** that the individual verdict is *hard to know* — that the ledger is long, the lives many, our sight short, and we should therefore be humble, hesitant, and inclined to charity. **That concession is worthless**, and it is the one that is always offered. It grants that the verdict is the right *kind* of thing to reach for and that we are merely poor at reaching. It leaves the instrument on the table.

**It is that the inference does not operate at that level at all.**

The *arthāpatti* was triggered by a fact about the **ensemble** — the unevenness of the distribution — and what it postulates is whatever is needed to make **the ensemble** stand. From *the distribution as a whole is ordered*, **no proposition whatever follows about this man.** Not a probable one. Not a weak one. **None.** The premise never mentioned him, and a conclusion cannot produce what its premise does not contain.

To move from the one to the other is not overreach. **It is changing the subject and failing to notice.**

---

## Two registers, and why this is not a counsel of humility

I want to make this general, because it is the load-bearing move of the essay and it will have to hold weight again in a moment.

There are **two registers** in which anything karmic can be said, and they have different objects.

**The ensemble register.** Statements about the distribution — that it is ordered, that it is not arbitrary, that it depends on something and not on nothing. This is the register of Brahmasūtra 2.1.34, and at this register the sūtra is sound, and I accept it.

**The individual register.** Statements about *this* trajectory, at *this* locus: what this man did, what he is owed, what has ripened in him.

And here is the claim: **an inference licensed at the ensemble register is not merely *weak* at the individual register. It is not a claim at the individual register at all.**

This is not epistemology. It is not that we lack the data. **It is a category distinction.** The two registers quantify over different things — one over a distribution, one over an element — and there is no rule of any logic that descends from the first to the second. A statement about the shape of a whole is not an under-specified statement about a part. It is a statement about something else.

The man who says *"the distribution is ordered, therefore his birth is his verdict"* has not made a bold inference on thin evidence. **He has made no inference.** He has uttered two sentences about two different subjects and put a *therefore* between them.

And notice: there is a **test** that separates the registers, and it is not a matter of taste.

**Ensemble-register statements are answerable at the ensemble.** Is the distribution ordered? That is a question you can press on: the *arthāpatti* holds only as long as the unevenness genuinely will not stand without the posit, and a better account of the unevenness would kill it. It is at risk. It could lose.

**Individual-register statements must be answerable at the individual.** And *this* is where the nature-answer collapses, and I will show it presently, because there is nothing — not one thing — that could ever happen to a man that would count against the verdict pronounced on him at his birth.

---

## And now a matter of plain logic, which is fatal on its own

Set the registers aside for a moment. Grant the śruti entirely. Let Chāndogya 5.10.7 be true exactly as it stands.

**What does it say?**

*Foul conduct* → *foul womb*. It runs **forward**: from the deed to the birth.

**What is it used to say?**

*This is a low birth* → *therefore, foul conduct*. It runs **backward**: from the birth to the deed.

**Those are not the same claim, and the second does not follow from the first.**

Every student in this tradition learns why in his first month, because the whole of Indian logic is built on it. An inference requires a ***vyāpti*** — an invariable pervasion — and **a *vyāpti* runs in one direction only** unless the other has been separately established.

The standard case. **Smoke proves fire.** Wherever there is smoke there is fire. You have seen the kitchen a thousand times; the pervasion holds; the inference is sound.

Now run it backwards. **Does fire prove smoke?**

**No.** And the counter-example is the one every student is made to memorise: the ***ayogolaka***, the **red-hot iron ball**. It is fire. There is no smoke. **One case is enough.** The backward *vyāpti* is dead, and it is dead forever, and no amount of further fires will revive it.

So: even granting in full that foul conduct leads to a foul birth, **you have not established that a foul birth comes only from foul conduct**. For that you would need to have shown that there is no other road to it — that in the whole of saṃsāra there is not one *ayogolaka*, not one low birth arriving by any other route whatever.

Nobody has shown that. Nobody has attempted it. **Nobody could.**

**The verdict runs the *vyāpti* backwards and calls the result knowledge.**

---

## The audit, instrument by instrument

**Pratyakṣa?** No. Not one person, ever, has perceived a single deed of a single prior life. Nothing was seen.

**Anumāna?** No — and not weakly, but structurally. Inference needs a *vyāpti*, and a *vyāpti* is built from **observed instances**. Here there are **zero**. Not few. Not scarce. **Zero.** No one has ever observed a life and then observed its next birth, so there is no positive instance and no negative one, nothing to generalise from and nothing to test against. **This is not a bad inference. It is not an inference.**

**Upamāna?** Nothing to compare it to.

**Arthāpatti?** It runs — and it stops at the ensemble, and it cannot descend.

**Śabda?** It gives a **forward** mechanism, and the verdict is a **backward** reading of it, and the backward reading is invalid on the tradition's own logic. The śruti is not being cited. It is being **reversed**.

**Anupalabdhi?** And here is where the whole thing gives itself away.

---

## The test, and what fails it

*Anupalabdhi* — the instrument by which an absence is known — carries a condition, and without the condition it is worthless. The condition is ***yogyatā***: **fitness to appear.**

You may assert that the pot is absent **only because the pot is of a kind that would have been apprehended had it been there.** Strike that condition out and the instrument dies on the spot, because then any man could announce the absence of anything at all, in any room he liked, and never once be wrong.

That condition is the sharpest thing in the epistemology, and it generalises into a single question that can be asked of **any** claim whatever:

**What, exactly, was apt to appear — and failed to?**

Now ask it of a load.

Something was **apt to happen** because you were holding it. The channel was to be cut and the water was coming. **You let go, and something specific failed to happen, and something specific fell.** The field floods. The books are wrong. The armies do not stop.

**You can be wrong about a load, and the world will tell you.** Reach for one that is not yours and you will find nothing under your hands, or you will find a man carrying it perfectly well and you standing in his way. Drop one you did not know you had, and you will **hear it land**.

**A load has a counterpart. It has a date. It is falsifiable. It is answerable.**

Now ask the same question of a nature-verdict.

**What would have to happen for it to be false?**

He prospers: merit, ripening.
He suffers: demerit, ripening.
He rises above his station: a rare good residue, surfacing.
He never rises: the residue, running its course.
He is more virtuous than anyone near him: then he was owed this and is working it off.
He is vicious: then you always knew.

**There is no result the world could produce that anybody was ever prepared to accept as a refusal.** Nothing was **apt to appear** whose failure to appear would have brought it down. The verdict was never, at any moment, at risk.

**And a claim that nothing could refuse is not a claim about the world.**

There is nothing here for any instrument to grip. Not because the instrument is too weak — because there is **no purchase**. *Anupalabdhi* does not fail to deliver the verdict. *Anupalabdhi* reports that the verdict is **not the sort of thing it delivers**.

**Six instruments. Not one of them reaches it.**

A civilisation that would not let a man assert the presence of a pot without naming his *pramāṇa* permitted him to assert the moral history of another man's soul on the strength of **nothing whatever** — and then built a settlement on the assertion and called the settlement dharma.

---

## The nature-answer tries to come back one storey up, and fails for the same reason

There is a repair available and it is the intelligent one, and I would make it if I were on the other side.

*Very well. Do not read the verdict off his birth. Read it off his **nature**. His* svabhāva *is what his prior karma made it — and* that *is what fixes his* svadharma. *The station is downstream of the nature, and the nature is downstream of the deed.*

This is better. It moves the claim off the crude marker and onto something that at least sounds like a fact about the man.

**And it fails at precisely the same joint, and it fails for the reason we already have in hand.**

Grant, at the **ensemble** register, that natures are karmically conditioned — that the distribution of *svabhāvas* across all beings is ordered and not arbitrary. I have already granted the ensemble claim once, at Brahmasūtra 2.1.34, and I grant it again.

**Nothing follows at the individual register.** From *natures, as a class, are karmically conditioned*, you cannot derive *this man's nature is his verdict*. It is the identical descent, over the identical gap, and it is void for the identical reason: **the premise is about a distribution and the conclusion is about an element, and there is no rule that takes you from one to the other.**

And run the test. **What would have to happen for a claim about this man's karmically-formed nature to be false?**

Nothing. Again nothing. **No *yogyatā*.** The claim has moved one storey up and acquired **not one particle** of purchase on the world.

**A verdict does not become knowledge by being restated at a higher altitude.**

---

## What the Gītā gives as the criterion

Now — what does the text actually say, when it says anything?

> चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं **गुणकर्मविभागशः** ।
> तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम् ॥
>
> *cāturvarṇyaṃ mayā sṛṣṭaṃ **guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ** |*
> *tasya kartāram api māṃ viddhy akartāram avyayam ||*
>
> "The fourfold order was brought forth by me **according to the distribution of *guṇa* and *karma***. Know me to be its author — and also to be no author, and unchanging."
>
> — Bhagavadgītā 4.13

> ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियविशां शूद्राणां च परंतप ।
> कर्माणि प्रविभक्तानि **स्वभावप्रभवैर्गुणैः** ॥
>
> *brāhmaṇakṣatriyaviśāṃ śūdrāṇāṃ ca paraṃtapa |*
> *karmāṇi pravibhaktāni **svabhāvaprabhavair guṇaiḥ** ||*
>
> "The works of brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras are distributed **by the *guṇas* arising from their own nature**."
>
> — Bhagavadgītā 18.41

***Guṇa.* *Karma.* *Svabhāva.*** And **not once**, in either verse, *janma*.

Now, someone will say: *but* svabhāva *means the nature you were born with, so this is birth by another name.*

**That is the very thing that has just failed the audit**, and I am not going to let it back in through a gloss. To read *svabhāva* as birth-indexed, you must already know that a man's nature is fixed by a verdict on a life nobody observed — and there is **no instrument that delivers that**, at any register, at any altitude. The gloss does not report what the word means. **It smuggles in the conclusion and calls it philology.**

So the position is clean, and it does not require the text to be flattering:

**The criterion the text states is not birth. The tradition substituted birth for it. A substitution requires a warrant. There is none.**

Not in *pratyakṣa*. Not in *anumāna*. Not in *arthāpatti*, which stops at the ensemble. Not in *śabda*, which runs forward and was read backwards.

There is no warrant, and there never was one.

## Manu could not hold it either

And here is the strangest confirmation, and it comes from the most rigid text in the whole corpus.

> अजीवंस्तु यथोक्तेन ब्राह्मणः स्वेन कर्मणा ।
> जीवेत्क्षत्रियधर्मेण स ह्यस्य प्रत्यनन्तरः ॥
>
> *ajīvaṃs tu yathoktena brāhmaṇaḥ svena karmaṇā |*
> *jīvet kṣatriyadharmeṇa sa hy asya pratyanantaraḥ ||*
>
> "A brāhmaṇa **unable to live** by his own prescribed occupation **may live by the dharma of a kṣatriya**, for that is the one next below him."
>
> — Manusmṛti 10.81

> उभाभ्यामप्यजीवंस्तु कथं स्यादिति चेद्भवेत् ।
> कृषिगोरक्षमास्थाय जीवेद्वैश्यस्य जीविकाम् ॥
>
> *ubhābhyām apy ajīvaṃs tu kathaṃ syād iti ced bhavet |*
> *kṛṣigorakṣam āsthāya jīved vaiśyasya jīvikām ||*
>
> "And if it be asked, *how is he to live when he cannot live by either of those?* — **let him take up ploughing and cattle-keeping, and live by the livelihood of a vaiśya.**"
>
> — Manusmṛti 10.82

This is *āpad-dharma*, the dharma of distress, and it is read as a kindly exception and passed over.

**It is not an exception. It is a confession, and it is decisive.**

Look at what has been conceded. The man cannot eat, so he may do a kṣatriya's work. Still cannot eat, so he may do a vaiśya's. **And the reason given is not that his nature has changed.** Nobody claims it has. The reason given is that **he must eat**.

Now hold that against the nature-answer. If the brāhmaṇa's duty flowed from **what he is** — from an essence delivered at birth by a verdict on an unremembered life — then **no famine could license altering it.** You cannot suspend a *nature* because a man is hungry. A thing is what it is, or it is not. **Essences do not take leave of absence.**

That Manu permits the exchange — **and permits it on grounds of subsistence** — is an admission, extracted under pressure from the most inflexible authority we possess, that the allocation is **administrative**. It is a posting. It is a **load-assignment**, it can be re-assigned, Manu re-assigns it — and in the moment he does, the essence-claim is gone and cannot be recovered.

Whatever *varṇa* was, **it was not a nature.** Its own chief legislator could not treat it as one for the length of a bad harvest.

## The verse that is supposed to settle it, and does not

> श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् ।
> स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः ॥
>
> *śreyān svadharmo viguṇaḥ paradharmāt svanuṣṭhitāt |*
> *svadharme nidhanaṃ śreyaḥ paradharmo bhayāvahaḥ ||*
>
> "Better one's own dharma, badly done, than another's dharma well performed. Better to die in one's own dharma; another's dharma brings danger."
>
> — Bhagavadgītā 3.35

Read *sva* as **fixed by your father**, and the verse holds a man in place for life.

Read *sva* as **fixed by what is actually resting on you**, and it says something else, and something considerably harder: **do not pick up a load that is not yours.** Do not take another man's burden because it looks nobler than the one under your hands. That is a warning against grandiosity, not against movement.

**The verse does not settle which reading is correct.** It cannot. It does not contain the information. Everything hangs on what fixes *sva* — and that question is settled by the audit, and the audit gives birth **no standing whatever**.

---

## Why I expect this argument to fail

One more thing, and I mark it clearly: **it is not part of the argument.** It proves nothing and it is not offered as evidence. It is only my account of why I do not expect the argument to work.

**A load has to be found.** You must look at the man. At the hour. At what is actually resting on him, and at what would fall if he let go. That is work. It must be done afresh for each person; it must be redone when circumstances change; you can get it wrong; and when you get it wrong the world will show you, and then you must go and look again.

**A rank spares you all of it.** You know his duty from his father's name. **You never have to look at him.** Not once. Not if you live beside him for forty years.

That is what it is **for**. It is a labour-saving device, and it saves the most expensive labour there is, which is attention.

Which is why no argument has ever dislodged it, and why this one will not either. **It is not held because it is believed. It is held because it is cheap.** And cheapness does not answer to *pramāṇa*, and never has, and has no intention of starting now.

---

## And so, at the end, two different men are reading this

I have been careful all the way through, and I am not going to become careless in the last two paragraphs, so I have to say something different to each of them.

**To the one who has been holding the rank** — reading other men off their fathers and calling the reading dharma — there is nothing gentler than what has already been said. The instrument you have been using was never an instrument. It delivered no knowledge. It was never at risk of being wrong, and **a thing that cannot be wrong cannot be right either**. What you had was not a finding. It was a shadow, and you cast it, and you have been reading it for a very long time.

**To the one who has been carrying it**, the finding is not the same finding, and I will not have it delivered to him as though it were one more debt.

Because the load reading does not merely take the rank away. **It takes away the telling.**

A rank is **told to you**. It is announced at your birth, in your absence, by other people, and nothing that happens for the rest of your life is permitted to disturb it.

**A load has to be found.** You look at the hour. You look at what is actually under your hands. You test it — you let go a little, and you see whether anything falls. **Nobody can hand it to you, and nobody can hand it to you wrong, because the world will correct them, and it will correct you, and being correctable is the whole of what makes it real.**

**What was on you was never yours to be told. Go and find what is.**

---

The wheel does not know what your father was.

It knows what is resting on you, at this hour, and whether you are still holding it. That is a hard thing, and it lets nobody off — and it is not the same hard thing that we were handed.

**A load is dated, and specific, and it pushes back. Drop it, and something falls, and you can hear it fall.**

**A rank has never pushed back once. Nothing casts it but the mouth that says it.**

Set it down. It was never in your hands.
